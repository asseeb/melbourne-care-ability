Life Stage and Transition Support in South East Melbourne

A big change can arrive before anyone feels ready. The last year of school, a first job, a move out of home, or a shift in who you rely on can leave days unstructured and families unsure what comes next. Life stage and transition support is NDIS mentoring that helps you prepare for that change.

Melbourne Care Ability works with participants across South East Melbourne who want a practical plan for the next stage of life. We focus on your goals, not a standard timetable. You choose what to practise, who comes to meetings, and how fast things move. Support coordinators and families are welcome when you want them there.

What is life stage transition support, and why you need it

Assistance with life stage transition is support and mentoring through a change in life circumstances. Typical moments include leaving school, starting work or study, moving house, becoming more independent, or adjusting after a change in family support.

The risk at these moments is not only the new task. It is the gap: a timetable that disappears, a service that ends, or a skill that has never been practised in real life. Transition support fills that gap with coaching, planning, and practice while the change is happening.

It is different from a course that teaches skills in general. Life skills development may sit alongside it. Transition support is tied to the change you are in, with mentoring that fits that stage.

Our life stage transition process

  1. 1

    Step 1: Hear the change

    We ask what is ending, what is starting, and what worries you most. School, work, home, and relationships can all be part of the same transition.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Set goals in your words

    We write a small set of goals the way you say them. A goal might be catching a bus to a course, speaking to a new manager, or spending a first night in a new home.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Mentor and practise

    A worker mentors you through the real steps. Practice happens in the places the change will actually occur, where that is safe and you agree.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Handover and review

    As the new stage settles, we review what still needs support and what you now do yourself. The service agreement is updated if the goal changes.

Key features and what is included

  • Mentoring through leaving school, starting work or study, or moving home
  • Goals written in your words, with a pace you agree to
  • Practice in real settings, such as travel, interviews, or a new household routine
  • Help to prepare for meetings with schools, employers, or housing services
  • Support to involve family or a coordinator only as you choose
  • Plain-language plans you can revisit
  • A service agreement before mentoring starts
  • A review when the transition settles or the goal shifts

Frequently asked questions

Who is life stage transition support for?

It is for NDIS participants facing a change such as leaving school, starting work or further study, moving out, or adjusting to a new level of independence. It can also help when family circumstances change and daily support needs to be rebuilt. It must be included in the plan.

Is this the same as life skills training?

They are related, and some people use both. Life skills development builds everyday skills such as cooking, budgeting, and travel. Life stage transition support is mentoring through a particular change, with goals tied to that stage. We can help you and your coordinator decide which support fits.

Can you attend school or employment meetings with me?

Yes, when that is part of the agreed support and the school or employer is willing. You decide what we say in the room. We do not make decisions for you.

How long does transition support last?

It lasts for the period your plan and agreement cover, and while the transition still needs mentoring. Some changes settle in a few months. Others need longer. We review rather than assuming the same hours forever.

Can a parent refer a young person?

A family member can enquire. We still need the participant involved in decisions in the way that is right for them, and we follow the consent arrangements in the plan. For anyone under 18, we work with parents or guardians as required.
