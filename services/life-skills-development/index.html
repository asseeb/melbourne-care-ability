Life Skills Development in South East Melbourne

Knowing you should be able to cook a meal or catch a bus is not the same as being able to do it on a Tuesday when you are tired. Skill gaps show up in real kitchens, real shops, and real conversations, and they shrink independence faster than people expect. Life skills development is NDIS support to build those everyday skills through practice.

Melbourne Care Ability helps participants across South East Melbourne learn and practise daily living skills such as cooking, budgeting, travel, and speaking up for themselves. Lessons follow your goals and your culture. We do not run you through a generic workbook. We practise the skill in the place you will use it, at a pace you set.

What is life skills development, and why you need it

Development of daily living and life skills is capacity-building support. A worker helps you learn, practise, and strengthen skills you want for a more independent day. Common areas are cooking, shopping, budgeting, using transport, personal organisation, and self-advocacy.

People need this support when they want to do more for themselves, or when a life change means an old support should step back. Practising with someone who will not take over the task is what makes the skill stick.

It is different from having a worker do the task for you. Household tasks and personal care get the job done. Life skills support is there so you can do more of the job yourself over time. Transition mentoring is another support, used when a specific life change is the reason for the coaching.

Our life skills development process

  1. 1

    Step 1: Choose the skill

    We ask which skill would change your week, and what you can already do.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Break it into steps

    We write a small sequence you agree with, in plain language.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Practise in real places

    Cooking happens in your kitchen. Travel practice happens on your route. Budgeting uses your actual bills, with only the detail you choose to share.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Review independence

    We look at what you now do alone and whether the hours should reduce, continue, or shift to a new skill.

Key features and what is included

  • Coaching in cooking, shopping, budgeting, and household organisation
  • Travel and public transport practice on routes you use
  • Self-advocacy practice for meetings and everyday decisions
  • Skills taught in your home or community, not only in a classroom
  • Pace and privacy set by you
  • Progress described in your words
  • A service agreement that names the skills
  • Reviews that can reduce support when you want more independence

Benefits of choosing us for life skills

  • Practice, not theory

    Skills are built where you will use them.

  • No takeover

    Workers prompt and step back. They do not quietly do the task to save time.

  • Cultural fit

    Food, money, family roles, and language are part of how skills are taught.

  • A local patch

    Routes, shops, and kitchens are in South East Melbourne, so practice transfers to your real week.

Frequently asked questions

What skills can you help me learn?

Common skills are cooking, shopping, cleaning routines, budgeting, catching public transport, using a phone or calendar, and speaking up in appointments. The skills we work on are the ones written into your agreement. We will not add a skill you did not choose.

Will I lose support if I get better at the skill?

The aim is more independence, and hours should change when you want them to. Getting better does not mean we abandon you mid-plan. We review with you and your coordinator so support steps down only when that is safe and agreed.

Do you handle my money?

We can coach you to budget and to understand a bill. We do not take control of your bank account, PIN, or Centrelink access. Do not share passwords with a worker.

Is this support for school leavers only?

No. Any funded participant who wants to build a daily living skill can use it, including adults who are changing homes, jobs, or routines. School leavers may also need life stage transition support.

Can we practise in my language?

Tell us the language you want used for learning. We will match a worker where we can, or agree how an interpreter or family member fits, with your consent.
